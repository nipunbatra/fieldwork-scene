How this was made

Nipun Batra selected the research task for Sustainability Lab. OpenAI Codex built this viewer and orchestrated the workflow. GPT-6 Astra labeled the images through Codex app-server using the user’s ChatGPT/Codex subscription, with recorded reasoning setting.

Two captures, two views

The Scene tab shows kiln footprints, fields, roads, vegetation, buildings, and other major regions. The Field burning tab shows field parcels grouped by visual burn appearance. The field view can use a nine-tile November 2020 seasonal study or the full 100-tile October 2023 scene. Changing the capture changes both the imagery and its corresponding labels; labels from different dates are never overlaid together.

Imagery dates and provenance

High map zoom does not imply new detail: zooms above level 18 enlarge the underlying imagery. Metadata resolution and location accuracy describe the source imagery, not annotation accuracy. Dates outside the study area can differ.

What the field labels mean

  • Burned-looking: predominantly char-like or ash-like appearance in the visible parcel.
  • Partially burned-looking: a visible mixture of apparent burn patches and other cover inside the same parcel.
  • Unburned-looking: no visible burn evidence in this capture; it does not mean the field has never burned.
  • Uncertain: dark soil, moisture, shadows, ploughing, residue, or image quality make the interpretation ambiguous.

No fire event, crop species, event date, pollution level, fire severity, or independently measured burned fraction is established. No before/after or infrared burn-index validation has been performed.
